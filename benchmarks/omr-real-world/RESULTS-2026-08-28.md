# Re-running the May 2026 real-world validation — what it found

**Date:** 2026-08-28
**Reproduce:** `python3 benchmarks/omr-real-world/rerun_baseline.py`

The May measurement in [README.md](README.md) was the last whole-pipeline check.
Everything in the three months since — the time-signature layer, the five
internal-consistency checks, clef and key-signature geometry, the body-text filter,
staff recovery, system grouping, slots, instrument identity — had only been measured on
narrow component slices. This asked the question those slices cannot: does a whole score
come out better?

---

## 0. The May table is not a usable baseline

**It records no settings** — no DPI, no `imgsz`, no confidence threshold. That is the
first finding, and it invalidated the first attempt at this comparison.

- **DPI.** Running at `transcribe`'s CLI default of 600 read as a catastrophic
  regression (Beethoven 5 noteheads 904 → 20,270). It was rendering resolution:
  bach-wtc p5 gives **496** noteheads at 300 dpi against May's 445, and 686 at 600.
  DPI 300 inferred and now hardcoded in the rerun script.
- **`imgsz`.** Neither setting reproduces May. On handel-reduction p20 at 300 dpi:
  **246** noteheads at `imgsz=1280`, **2799** at `imgsz=2048`, against May's **476**.

Record the settings with the numbers. A benchmark that cannot be reproduced cannot be
regressed against.

## 1. `imgsz=2048` over-detects by ~11x on narrow cells

The `imgsz` swing above is not a tuning detail — it is a bug, and the pipeline's own CLI
default is on the wrong side of it.

Six consecutive cells of handel-reduction p20 were rendered and **counted by eye**:
4 + 1 + 3 + 1 + 4 + 3 = **16 noteheads across 6 cells, 2.7 per cell**.

| setting | noteheads | per cell | verdict |
|---|--:|--:|---|
| `imgsz=1280` | 246 | **2.6** | matches the count |
| `imgsz=2048` | 2799 | 29.2 | ~11x over |

**Mechanism.** Cells are canonically rescaled before detection, so `imgsz` sets an
*upscale ratio*, not an absolute size. A wide cell is barely upscaled; a narrow one is
blown up enormously and the detector fires on texture. That predicts the split we see —
bach-wtc has 28 wide keyboard cells and comes to 17.7 noteheads/cell, which is plausible
for busy baroque, while handel-reduction has 96 narrow cells and blows up.

So `imgsz` should be **matched to cell size**, not fixed. Note the two defaults already
disagree: `transcribe.py`'s CLI default is **2048**, while `CLAUDE.md` documents the web
app's `OMR_IMGSZ` default as **1280**. The CLI is the one producing the bad numbers.

## 2. Structure is stable, and correct where ground truth exists

At 300 dpi, `imgsz=2048` (the setting the rest of this table was run at, so treat the
notehead column as unreliable):

| score | systems May→now | staves May→now | cells May→now | pitch | rhythm |
|---|---|---|---|--:|--:|
| bach-wtc | 5 → 5 | 10 → 10 | 32 → **28** | 100% | 100% |
| handel-leadsheet | 3 → 3 | 15 → 15 | 60 → 55 | 100% | 100% |
| handel-reduction | 2 → 2 | 12 → 12 | 108 → 96 | 100% | 100% |
| ravel-bolero | 3 → **2** | 32 → 32 | 112 → 192 | 100% | 100% |
| beethoven-5 | 2 → 2 | 18 → **19** | 98 → 274 | 100% | 100% |

bach-wtc's 32 → 28 is a **correction, not a regression**: the hand-verified ground truth
is 3+2+3+3+3 = 14 bars x 2 staves = 28 cells. May's 32 came from an eyeballed 3+3+3+3+4
the page does not support.

## 3. The verification layer now carries signal

May had none of this. Now: **46** `phase1_warning` on Beethoven 5, **40**
`rhythm_sum_warning` on Boléro, **7** on the Handel lead sheet — measures the pipeline
itself flags as internally contradictory, rather than emitting silently.

## 4. On the densest scores, no clef is read at all

| score | clef sources |
|---|---|
| bach-wtc | 4 detector, 6 DEFAULTED |
| handel-leadsheet | 2 detector, 3 cv_locator, 10 DEFAULTED |
| **handel-reduction** | **12/12 DEFAULTED** |
| ravel-bolero | 20 detector, 2 cv_locator, 10 DEFAULTED |
| **beethoven-5** | **19/19 DEFAULTED** |

Every pitch on those two pages rests on a positional guess. The geometry layer shipped
today is not reaching them, and they are exactly the pages without a PDF text layer — so
the instrument-identity route to fixing their clefs needs the vision margin reader
(`benchmarks/omr-margin-labels-2026-08/`).

## Method note

Both near-misses today were the same error: a number that looked decisive while
measuring the wrong thing — first a ground-truth-free proxy for system grouping, then
this DPI mismatch. Both were caught by rendering the thing and looking at it. The
notehead question was settled the same way, by counting six cells by eye.
