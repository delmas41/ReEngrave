# What can a mark be? — the elimination table, for Sean to correct

You asked whether the reader does general deduction: *"this is a mostly oval shaped note on the staff —
therefore it can only be a few things."* Today it does not. The detector names one thing per box, and
each later rule only asks "is it NOT that?"; if the answer is no, the mark is thrown away rather than
renamed.

This page is a first try at the deduction you described. Every mark gets four rough facts:

- **its size**, in staff spaces (a dot, a notehead-sized oval, a thin tall stroke, a flat stroke, a long
  curve, an accidental-sized glyph, a clef-sized glyph);
- **how much of its box is ink** (solid, hollow, a thin line);
- **where it stands up and down**: in the staff, just outside it (where ledger lines are), or far from
  any staff;
- **where it stands along the bar**: the clef/key slots at the start of a system, the head of a bar, the
  middle of a bar, on a barline, or in the left margin.

Each line below says what a mark of that shape, in that place, can still be. Everything else is ruled out.

**Please correct anything wrong.** If a list is missing something that really happens on the page, say
so — that is the most useful answer.

## How it did on your Brahms page (your 1,566 labelled marks)

- The right answer was still in the list for **1,537 of 1,566 marks (98%)**.
- For half of all marks, the list is **3 things or fewer**; stems and augmentation dots are almost
  always down to one.
- Accidentals, time-signature digits, rests and flags stay at 8-10 possibilities: size and place alone
  cannot tell a sharp from an 8 from a quarter rest.
- None of the 29 misses came from an engraving rule; all were my size or ink numbers being too tight
  (fixable), and two look like labels to check (questions 1 and 2 below).

## The lists

### A small round dot

- in the staff, mid-bar: dot (augmentation or staccato)
- just outside the staff (ledger range): dot (augmentation or staccato); text (word, label, direction)
- far from any staff: text (word, label, direction)
- in the clef/key slots: **nothing** (today the table would say unread)
- at the head of a bar: dot (augmentation or staccato)
- on a barline: dot (augmentation or staccato)
- in the left margin: text (word, label, direction)
