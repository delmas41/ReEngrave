# The `--direction-text` default, decided — ON (2026-09-02)

`docs/next-steps-omr-2026-09-01.md` §4b left this open, and named the trap in it
correctly: *"Surya helps on scans, not engravings"* is **true of the margin-label
reader**, whose first rung is the PDF text layer, and **false of the direction
reader**, which has no text-layer rung and for which Surya is the only reader of
the words. A scans-only default would have switched it off exactly where it was
measured helping.

The entry then named the missing measurement — the flag on a scan, end to end —
and that is what this is. It changed the answer twice on the way.

## What it is worth, on two trees

| tree | default | with the reader | difference |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| `bd88b32`, before the overnight integration | 0.1101 / 791 | 0.0883 / 647 | **−144** |
| `7e0f8ec`, after | 0.1066 / 767 | 0.0849 / 623 | **−144** |

Both rows were re-derived here rather than quoted; both reproduced the record to
the edit. `wrong direction` is 151 pooled edits, **19.7%**, the third-largest
bucket, and it leaves the reported top six entirely with the reader on.
`wrong note` (234) and `wrong flag/beam` (198) are identical in both
configurations — the flag moves its own bucket and nothing else.

## It is additive, and that was checked rather than assumed

A reader with perfect precision can still make output worse by putting a
correctly-read word on the wrong beat, and a pass that walks the page dict can
perturb something unrelated. So `eval_flag_on_scan.py` exports both
configurations and diffs them with every `<direction>` block removed.

| | placed → `<words>` | outside `<direction>` |
|---|---|---|
| Beethoven, engraved | 1 → 1 | identical |
| Brahms, engraved | 14 → 14 | identical |
| Mahler, engraved | 0 → 0 | identical |
| Beethoven 5 p.1, real scan | 0 → 0 | **byte-identical export** |

And on the merged tree, four direction-dense pages of the Litolff scan:

| page | candidates | read | accepted | placed → `<words>` | outside `<direction>` |
|---|--:|--:|--:|---|---|
| 16 | 20 | 18 | 7 | 7 → 6 | CLEAN |
| 22 | 13 | 12 | 7 | 7 → 7 | CLEAN |
| 39 | 35 | 35 | 10 | 10 → 10 | CLEAN |
| 78 | 40 | 38 | 9 | 9 → 8 | CLEAN |

**33 placed, 31 exported.** The two that vanish are a real bug and not this
flag's: a measure the detector found NO events in takes the whole-measure-rest
path, which never calls the function that emits `<direction>` — so it drops
directions and, since a month earlier, dynamics. Fixed on another branch
(`46e42a4`); the engraved benchmark cannot reach it, because the trigger is a
bar with nothing at all in it and a rest counts as an event.

⚠️ Flipping the default **raises that bug's priority**: it went from affecting
people who opted in to affecting everyone.

## The cost objection in the code was mostly not this flag's

`transcribe`'s comment gave the reason it was off:

> OFF by default because it spends OCR: ~9 s on a 21-staff page with the Surya
> server resident, and the first call of a run pays ~70 s more to spawn
> llama.cpp and load a 650M GGUF.

**The ~70 s belongs to whoever asks for Surya first**, and on any page without a
PDF text layer that is the margin-label reader — on by default since it shipped.
Measured on a scan with the direction reader OFF:

    label_tiers = {'text_layer': 0, 'surya': 12, 'tesseract': 0, ...}

The model is already resident before the direction reader is reached. What the
flag adds is the OCR of the word crops:

| page | candidates | `direction_text_s` | per crop |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| Mahler, engraved | few | 0.25 | — |
| Beethoven, engraved | — | 6.07 | — |
| Brahms, engraved | — | 11.69 | — |
| scan p.78, twice | 40, 40 | 21.5 / 20.1 | 0.54 / 0.50 |
| scan p.84, twice | 21, 21 | 15.6 / 17.5 | 0.74 / 0.83 |
| scan p.16 | 20 | 16.0 | 0.80 |
| scan p.22 | 13 | 15.0 | 1.15 |
| **scan p.39** | 35 | **177.6** | **5.07** |

⚠️ **The per-crop cost is not flat, and an earlier draft of this file said
0.5-0.8 s on the strength of two pages.** Across six scan pages it runs
**0.5 to 5.1 s**, and page 39 spends 177.6 s — three minutes of OCR on one page.
That is bounded and it is not small. It is still the right side of the decision
(a three-minute page is not a ten-minute one, and the page's own transcription
takes minutes anyway), but quote the range, not the floor.

Where the page HAS a text layer — Brahms and Mahler — the margin reader answers
from rung 1 and never loads Surya, so there the direction reader does pay the
load. It is once per run rather than per page.

⚠️ **Do not read a wall-clock total off a scan run.** The four-page merged-tree
run came out **592.9 s off → 564.8 s on**: the flag made the whole thing
*faster*, which is impossible if it only adds work. Page 16 alone went 440.3 s
off → 209.6 s on. The totals are dominated by the same page-level variance
`NONDETERMINISM_2026-09-02.md` documents, and `direction_text_s` is the only
attributable number here.

## ⚠️ Two measurements that were wrong, and would have decided it the other way

**The first cost figure said +127%, with one page at 606 s of OCR.** Ten minutes
on one page is not a default, and that nearly ended it. It was **contention** —
that run overlapped a benchmark eval and two control runs. Re-measured with
nothing else on the machine, the same page is 15.6 s and 17.5 s, and accepts 11
words both times.

What made the difference was checking the *mechanism* rather than accepting a
number that had a story attached. `SCAN_2026-09-01.md` documents Surya
generating 1307 characters of invented prose on one crop of this very page, so
"a runaway generation" was an available and plausible explanation. It is not
what happened: **page 84's longest rejected string is 14 characters.** A
measurement with a ready-made mechanism is the easiest kind to believe.

**The safety check reported the flag changing 485 lines outside every
`<direction>` — and that was not the flag either.** Same page, two runs of the
same configuration, same 485 lines. It is the margin-label readers, which are on
by default, and it is a bigger open problem than this decision was:
[NONDETERMINISM_2026-09-02.md](NONDETERMINISM_2026-09-02.md). It is also
orthogonal — the direction reader itself reproduces (page 78: 9 accepted twice;
page 84: 11 accepted twice).

## The decision, and what it rests on

**ON by default.** `read_direction_text=None` means "no opinion" and defers to
`OMR_DIRECTION_TEXT` (default `1`); an explicit `False` still wins, so a caller
is never overruled by a machine's environment. `--no-direction-text` on both
CLIs.

- worth 144 edits, 18.8% of the pooled figure, stable across two trees;
- additive on every page where the pipeline is stable, checked per page;
- 0.5–0.8 s per candidate crop, bounded and reproducible;
- degrades to nothing where no OCR rung exists — `read_directions` returns `[]`
  with `reason="no OCR rung available"`, which is tested;
- and its twin, the margin-label reader, has the identical dependency and has
  been on by default all along. This was the odd one out.

## What this does not settle

**The scan yield is low and this does not change that.** 17 accepted over five
direction-dense pages pre-merge, and 0 accepted on the one low-density scan page
measured end to end. The flag buys much less on scans than on engravings; what
the measurement shows is that it never costs correctness there, only time.

**Placement accuracy on a scan is unmeasured and unmeasurable here.** A scan has
no truth file, so nothing prices a correctly-read word attached to the wrong
beat — the failure mode that costs double on the engraved benchmark. The scan
evidence is about precision and additivity, not placement.
