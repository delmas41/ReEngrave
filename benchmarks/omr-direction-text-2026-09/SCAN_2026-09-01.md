# The direction reader on a real scan

`FINDINGS.md` ends by saying that nothing in this layer had met broken ink,
that the OCR's failure mode is silence, and that **the next honest measurement
is a scan, not a smaller residue on the engraved benchmark**. This is that
measurement.

**Corpus.** Beethoven 5, Henry Litolff's Verlag c.1870, IMSLP 575951 — a real
photographic scan of a 19th-century print, 87 pages, chosen because it carries a
PDF text layer that names what is printed. Five pages, picked for direction
density from that layer: 16 (the Andante opening), 22, 39, 78, 84. 96 staves and
74 candidates in total.

```bash
python3 benchmarks/omr-direction-text-2026-09/eval_on_scan.py \
    library/editions/beethoven/symphony-5-op67/beethoven--*imslp575951.pdf \
    --pages 16 22 39 78 84 --crops-dir /tmp/scan-crops
python3 benchmarks/omr-direction-text-2026-09/bakeoff_readers_on_scan.py \
    /tmp/scan-crops
```

## The headline: precision survives intact, recall does not

| | engraved benchmark | this scan |
|---|--:|--:|
| accepted readings | 15 | **44** |
| **wrong readings** | **0** | **0** |
| recall | 15 of 16 printed | **~66%** of what the text layer sees |

**Not one invented word in 74 candidates on 1870 paper.** Every accepted string
is text genuinely present in its own crop, checked by eye and corroborated by
two OCR engines reading the same crop independently.

**And recall falls from essentially total to about two thirds.** That is the
real result, and it took three passes to get there from the 18% the first
measurement showed. A reader that finds two thirds of the directions and never
invents one is useful; a reader whose rate was not known would be dangerous,
because the output looks the same either way.

This file records three passes, and the distance between them is the finding:

| pass | accepted | wrong |
|---|--:|--:|
| Surya alone | 12 | 0 |
| + the Tesseract union rung | 17 | 0 |
| **+ the band and span fixes below** | **44** | **0** |

Nothing was traded for it. Precision stayed at zero invented words through
every step, and the engraved benchmark did not move by a single edit through
any of them.

## Where the loss went, and it was not where the design expected

On the first pass the loss looked almost entirely OCR-side, not candidate-side:

| stage | on these five pages |
|---|---|
| candidates proposed | 74 |
| of those, containing ink a second OCR can read | **72** |
| read by Surya | 21 |
| accepted by the lexicon, Surya alone | 11 |
| accepted by the lexicon, both rungs | 17 |
| accepted after the band and span fixes | **44** |

That last row is the correction to this section's own conclusion. The candidate
step WAS also losing words — two bugs' worth, in "Page 22" below — and the
first pass could not see it because both faults look identical from here to an
OCR that is silent anyway.

The candidate CV — subtract the detections, refuse the curves by fill ratio,
cluster the letters — transfers to a scan largely intact. Inspecting page 16's
fourteen crops by eye: eleven are real printed text (`Andante con`, `moto.`,
`p dolce`, `dolce`, `arco. f`, `cresc. f`, four more `cresc.`) and three are
music ink. That is the part nobody would have bet on, and it held.

**Surya is silent on 53 of 74 crops that contain readable words.** Finding 5 of
`FINDINGS.md` recorded this failure mode on engraved pages, where it cost one
word and was fixed by upscaling. On a scan it is the dominant term.

Page 22 is the one exception and the one candidate-step failure: 4 candidates
against 7 direction words the layer sees, 0 accepted. **Opened, and it was two
bugs — see below. Page 22 now proposes 13 candidates and accepts all 7 of its
printed directions.**

## Surya hallucinated 1307 characters, and the lexicon is what stopped it

On one crop — a `sempre` on page 84, staff 8 — Surya returned:

> SERIALIZATION SERIALIZATION is a strategy to build a serial list of products
> and processes that are not to be consumed on a single basis. […]

repeated to **1307 characters** of fluent invented corporate prose. Surya 2 is a
language model behind an OCR interface (llama.cpp, a 650M GGUF), and on an
ambiguous crop it did what language models do.

`direction_lexicon` refused it, and it cost nothing. But it is worth stating
plainly, because the gate was justified on *arithmetic* — an invented word costs
what a missed one costs — and this is a much stronger reason than the
arithmetic: **the free reader can emit a page of confident nonsense, and the
lexicon is the only thing between that and the exported score.** Anyone tempted
to loosen the gate to buy recall should read this paragraph first.

## Tesseract: refuted as a replacement, shipped as a companion

The obvious lever, and one another session reached independently on a different
Beethoven 5 edition (IMSLP 984073, no text layer): Tesseract reads crops where
Surya is silent. That observation is **correct** — and it is not sufficient,
because reading a crop is not the same as being able to use it.

Both engines on the same 74 crops, the exact images the shipped reader saw:

| rung | crops read | **lexicon-accepted** |
|---|--:|--:|
| Surya (was shipped alone) | 21 | **11** |
| Tesseract, raw | 72 | **5** |
| Tesseract, staff-line fragments stripped | 72 | 11 |
| **Surya OR stripped Tesseract** | 73 | **17** |

**Swapping the rung would have LOST recall**: Tesseract reads three and a half
times as many crops and yields fewer usable words, because its errors are
in-word (`Crese.`, `CTeSC.`, `crese.£`) where Surya's are total. It converts
silence into noise, and the gate then refuses the noise. The measurement that
matters is not "does it read" but "does it read something the lexicon accepts",
and those rank the two engines oppositely.

Much of Tesseract's residue is one character class: `|`, `[`, `]` — staff lines
and barlines crossing the crop and read as punctuation. Stripping those recovers
`'|sempre |'`, `'| cresc.'` and `'[sempre |'` and brings it level with Surya by
a completely different route, which is what makes the union worth more than
either.

### Shipped, and measured on both corpora

`direction_text.default_readers` now asks both, in that order, and each rung
self-disables when its dependency is absent.

| | before (Surya only) | **after (union)** |
|---|--:|--:|
| scan: accepted | 12 | **17** |
| scan: wrong | 0 | **0** |
| scan: recall vs the text layer | 18% | **~37%** |
| engraved benchmark: pooled OMR-NED | 0.1624 | **0.1624** |
| engraved: `wrong direction` | 7 | **7** |

**The engraved benchmark does not move at all**, which is the result that
mattered most for shipping it. Surya already reads every crop there; Tesseract
adds only noise (`'ee ee ee ee ee'`, `'atoll y yt'`, `'(J = 108)'`) and the
lexicon refuses all of it. All fifteen engraved readings are still Surya's, with
zero conflicts. The union buys recall exactly where recall was missing and costs
nothing where it was not.

### What Tesseract actually contributed, which was not what was predicted

The five it supplied were expected to be crops Surya was silent on. Two were.
**Three were repairs** — Surya read the crop and got a letter wrong, the lexicon
refused it, and Tesseract read the same crop correctly:

| page / staff | Surya | Tesseract | kind |
|---|---|---|---|
| 39 / 13 | `''` | `poco rit` | fill |
| 78 / 15 | `''` | `poco` | fill |
| 78 / 12 | `'cresc. )'` | `cresc.` | **repair** |
| 84 / 0 | `'sempire'` | `sempre` | **repair** |
| 84 / 4 | `'senpre'` | `sempre` | **repair** |

So the two rungs are complementary in both directions, not merely
silence-and-fill. That is a better reason for the union than the one it was
adopted on.

### Precision after the union: still nothing invented

All 17 accepted strings are text genuinely present in their own crop, checked by
eye. Sixteen are complete words. The seventeenth, `poco rit` on page 39, is
worth stating exactly because it is easy to mistake for an OCR error: **the crop
itself is clipped** — it contains `poco rita` and no more — so Tesseract read it
faithfully and the truncation is candidate geometry, not reading. It would cost
a few edits against a truth that says `poco ritard.`, against twelve for missing
the mark entirely.

### Cost

**139 ms per crop, 10.3 s for all 74** — Tesseract is a local binary with no
model to load, against Surya's ~9 s per page with a resident server. The union
roughly doubles a page's OCR time and adds no new failure mode: a rung that
throws is caught, recorded in `failed_readers`, and the other one still answers.

One scale note found while timing: handing Surya all 74 crops in a single batch
exceeded its 900 s timeout. The pipeline reads per page (14–26 crops here), so
nothing in the shipped path is near it, but a caller batching a whole score
would be.

## Page 22: what one bad page was hiding

Page 22 proposed 4 candidates against 7 printed directions and accepted none.
Tracing each printed word through every stage — is it in a band, is it inside
the staff's x-range, what ink survives the subtraction, which components pass,
what cluster forms — found two bugs, both of which had been costing words on
every page and were merely most visible here.

### 1. The below-band was capped at 3 spaces when the gap is 7

`below_spaces` is documented as the reach "when nothing is under it — the last
staff of a system". The implementation applied it as a **universal cap**, so a
band never extended more than 3 staff spaces below its staff even when the next
staff was 7 spaces away and the whole gap was unambiguously that staff's.

Five of page 22's seven words sat past it. The trace says so plainly:

    'arco'   y 1606-1712   NOT IN ANY BAND
                           nearest: staff 3 below, y 1508-1591 (off by 2.2 spaces)

The engraved benchmark never showed this because LilyPond sets its directions
tight under the staff; a 19th-century engraver does not. **Inside a system the
gap between two staves has exactly one claimant**, so the band now runs the
whole way to the next staff. `below_spaces` reverts to what it was documented
as: the reach where nothing bounds the gap.

### 2. A slur's bounding box was erasing whole words

`sempre` on page 22 was in a band, inside the x-range, and had **nine
components before the subtraction and zero after**. A `slur` detected at
731 × 121 px — 24.2 × 4.0 staff spaces, confidence 0.48 — covered it, and the
subtraction blanks a detection's box.

Blanking a box is right for a glyph, where the box *is* the glyph, and wrong for
a span, whose box is the rectangle its arc travels through and is mostly paper.
The class space puts a clean gap there. Widest box in every category, measured
over one scanned and one engraved page, in staff spaces:

    notehead 3.2   clef 3.2   dynamic 2.6   flag 1.8   accidental 1.2
    ----------------------------------------------------------------
    pedal bracket 7.7   slur 24.2   staff 26.6   restHBar 36.9   beam 37.2

So a detection wider than 4 spaces is no longer blanked. **Nothing is lost by
leaving spans in**: their ink is either thread-thin or enormous, which is
exactly what `min_fill_ratio` and `max_glyph_width_spaces` refuse — the
fill-ratio test was written for slurs in the first place.

### And two the wider bands then exposed in the gate

Reaching further found more words and also more of the OCR's failure modes.
Both were precision faults, both in the lexicon, and both had the same shape:
a string that is garbage but whose every token is a real term.

- **A word repeated immediately.** One `arco.` came back from Surya as
  `arco. arco. arco. arco.` — a language model repeating itself, and every
  token a valid term. No direction repeats a word immediately, so the lexicon
  refuses it now. **Adjacent, not anywhere**: `poco a poco` is a real marking
  and repeats `poco` with a connective between.
- **English glue.** `the`, `and` and `of` were connectives. Their only measured
  effect was to pass a scanned `a Tempo.` misread as `a Tempo. and`. No
  direction in this repertoire is English; they are gone.

**Neither cost a single reading, and that is the interesting part.** In all
three cases the other rung had read the same crop correctly — `arco.`,
`a Tempo.` — so the gate refusing the bad string is precisely what lets the
union reach the right one. The count stayed at 44 while three wrong strings
became right ones. The gate and the union compose: a stricter gate makes the
second rung *more* valuable, not less.

### What the four fixes are worth

| | candidates | accepted | wrong |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| page 22 before | 4 | 0 | 0 |
| **page 22 after** | **13** | **7 of 7 printed** | **0** |
| five pages before | 74 | 17 | 0 |
| **five pages after** | **129** | **44** | **0** |

All 44 were checked against their own crops by eye. Every one is a genuine
printed direction. A few are partial where the crop is clipped — `Andante con`
for `Andante con moto.`, `poco ritard` for `poco ritard.` — which is candidate
geometry rather than reading, and costs a Levenshtein distance rather than a
whole word.

**The engraved benchmark did not move**: 0.1364 → 0.1138 before and after, the
same 14 Brahms readings and 1 Beethoven, all still Surya's, zero conflicts. Its
candidate count rose 17 → 20 and every extra was refused by the lexicon
(`'Sf tot ttt'`, `'So 4+'`, `'la'`).

## Is the second rung worth its keep? Re-asked after everything else changed

The union was adopted when Surya alone accepted 11 of 74 crops. Everything since
— the band fix, the span fix, two lexicon tightenings — changed what each rung is
shown and what the gate does with it, so the question was re-asked from scratch
on the current code rather than assumed to still hold (`union_worth.py`):

| | accepted, five scan pages |
|---|--:|
| Surya alone | 23 |
| **both rungs** | **44** |

**The second rung supplies 21 of the 44 — nearly half the yield**, and it is
worth more now than when it was adopted, not less, because the band fix handed
both rungs many more legible crops and Surya is silent on most of them.

**It cannot make a reading worse, and that is structural rather than lucky.**
Surya has precedence, so the second rung is consulted only where Surya's string
was empty or refused. Across all 129 candidates there is exactly one crop where
both accept and name different words — `Tempo.` against `, Tempo.` — and
precedence takes Surya's, which is the right one.

Of the 21 it adds, 11 are crops Surya said nothing about and **10 are repairs**:

    surya 'sempire'   'senpre'   'scripte'   "s'enupre."   'senspre.'   -> sempre
    surya 'Farco.'    'cresc. )'  'a Tempo. and'                        -> arco. cresc. a Tempo.
    surya 'arco. arco. arco.'  'arco. arco. arco. arco.'                -> arco.

Those last two lines are worth reading twice, because they are the strings that
looked like the union misbehaving and are the opposite. `arco. arco. arco.` and
`a Tempo. and` are **Surya's** output, not Tesseract's — the language-model rung
repeating itself and the lexicon's English glue letting it through. Tesseract
read the same crops correctly. Tightening the gate against those strings did not
cost a single reading precisely because the second rung was there to answer
instead.

**On the engraved benchmark the union is provably a no-op.** Every accepted
reading there is Surya's (`by_reader` = `{surya: 14}` and `{surya: 1}`) and there
are no conflicts, so removing the second rung could not change one of them. Its
noise — `'Sf tot ttt'`, `'So 4+'` — is refused by the lexicon and never reaches
the score.

### The page decides, not the caller

Since the second rung is worth 21 readings on a scan and provably 0 on a
born-digital page, the page is asked which it is. It costs milliseconds and
reads the PDF's own structure — an engraver emits the notes as VECTOR PATHS, a
scanner emits one raster covering the sheet:

|  | vector paths | images | image cover |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| engraved (3 fixtures) | 467–2058 | **0** | 0.00 |
| scanned (14 IMSLP editions) | 0–1 | **1–2** | 0.86–1.41 |

Two populations that do not touch, on a signal available before anything is
rendered. Confirmed end to end: the engraved benchmark now runs ONE rung and
scores 0.1138, identical to running two, while scan pages 22 and 84 run both
and Tesseract supplies 1 and 5 of their readings respectively.

⚠️ **The obvious signal does not work, which is worth recording so nobody
re-derives it.** Print noise ought to show as mid-grey pixels a vector render
has none of. It does not separate them at all — 0.098–0.216 for the engraved
pages against 0.009–0.978 for the scans. Embedded FONTS are no better: several
scans carry nine, for a text layer someone attached later.

**It must PROVE born-digital, and that asymmetry is the design.** A page wrongly
called scanned costs 140 ms a crop. A page wrongly called engraved loses the
second reader on material where it supplies half the readings, and loses it
silently. So a hybrid, a blank page, or a PDF that will not open is treated as a
scan, and the classifier may only ever drop the SECOND rung — never the first,
which would leave a page unread rather than merely read once.

⚠️ **And the claim it acts on is thin.** "The second rung is worthless on
born-digital pages" is measured on three LilyPond fixtures, which is no basis at
all for a statement about every engraver. A born-digital edition whose font
defeats Surya would show up as candidates proposed and not accepted with
`readers` naming one rung — which is why the report records both the
classification and the rungs that ran.

### The one real cost, and the switch for it

About **140 ms a crop**, so roughly double a page's OCR time. Nothing else: no
accuracy cost anywhere measured, on either corpus.

`OMR_DIRECTION_READERS=surya` restricts the reader to one rung, so a caller who
measures the second worth less than its time on their own material can switch it
off without editing code — and so this comparison stays reproducible on a corpus
nobody here has seen.

## How the numbers were arrived at, including two ways they were wrong first

Recall against a text layer is a floor, not a rate — the layer is another OCR
(it reads `Fag:` for `Fag.` and makes `tJ` out of noteheads), it holds margin
labels and page furniture this reader never looks at, and it counts a word once
per staff so a `sempre` on twelve staves is twelve entries. The 67 is
direction-words whose centre falls inside some staff's own x-range.

Two matching bugs were caught before any of this was believed, both mine:

1. **Matching on x alone.** Reported zero corroboration on page 16 where both
   readers had independently found the same two `cresc.` — a page number four
   staves away shared their column. On a 20-staff page every x is crowded.
   Matching is 2-D now.
2. **Geometric matching without content.** A 3-space window called correct
   readings uncorroborated at 3.8 spaces, because the layer boxes a whole word
   and this reader boxes the letter cluster it kept. The final numbers require
   the letters to agree as well as the position.

Both failures had the same shape as the pooled-versus-per-instance mistake in
`FINDINGS.md` finding 6: a summary statistic that looked authoritative and was
measuring the wrong thing. The crops settled it in both cases.

## What this does not say

- **Nothing here is about a different print.** One edition, five pages, one
  century, one typeface. The engraved benchmark's lesson — that a threshold
  which travels between corpora is rare — applies to this file too.
- **Nothing here measures placement.** A scan has no truth for which beat a mark
  belongs to; every number above is about whether the word is found and read.
- **The 18% is not a bug to be fixed by tuning.** It is one engine being silent
  on legible input. The union above is the measured response; a different vision
  rung (the paid one, already wired for margin labels) is the untested one.
