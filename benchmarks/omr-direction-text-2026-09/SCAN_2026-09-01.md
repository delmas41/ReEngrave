# The direction reader on a real scan

`FINDINGS.md` ends by saying that nothing in this layer had met broken ink,
that the OCR's failure mode is silence, and that **the next honest measurement
is a scan, not a smaller residue on the engraved benchmark**. This is that
measurement.

**Corpus.** Beethoven 5, Henry Litolff's Verlag c.1870, IMSLP 575951 — a real
photographic scan of a 19th-century print, 87 pages, chosen because it carries a
PDF text layer that names what is printed. Five pages, picked for direction
density from that layer: 16 (the Andante opening), 22, 39, 78, 84. 96 staves and
74 candidates in total.

```bash
python3 benchmarks/omr-direction-text-2026-09/eval_on_scan.py \
    library/editions/beethoven/symphony-5-op67/beethoven--*imslp575951.pdf \
    --pages 16 22 39 78 84 --crops-dir /tmp/scan-crops
python3 benchmarks/omr-direction-text-2026-09/bakeoff_readers_on_scan.py \
    /tmp/scan-crops
```

## The headline: precision survives intact, recall collapses

| | engraved benchmark | this scan |
|---|--:|--:|
| accepted readings | 15 | **17** |
| **wrong readings** | **0** | **0** |
| recall | 15 of 16 printed | **~37%** of what the text layer sees |

**Not one invented word in 74 candidates on 1870 paper.** Every accepted string
is text genuinely present in its own crop, checked by eye and corroborated by
two OCR engines reading the same crop independently.

**And recall falls from essentially total to about a third.** That is the real
result. A reader that finds a third of the directions and never invents one is
useful; a reader whose 37% was not known would be dangerous, because the output
looks the same either way.

The first pass of this measurement ran Surya alone and scored **12 accepted,
18% recall**. The gap between those two rows is the union rung this file
motivated and now records — see "Tesseract" below. Both numbers are kept
because the difference between them is the finding.

## Where the 82% goes, and it is not where the design expected

The loss is almost entirely in the OCR, not in the candidate step:

| stage | on these five pages |
|---|---|
| candidates proposed | 74 |
| of those, containing ink a second OCR can read | **72** |
| read by Surya | 21 |
| accepted by the lexicon, Surya alone | 11 |
| accepted by the lexicon, both rungs | **17** |

The candidate CV — subtract the detections, refuse the curves by fill ratio,
cluster the letters — transfers to a scan largely intact. Inspecting page 16's
fourteen crops by eye: eleven are real printed text (`Andante con`, `moto.`,
`p dolce`, `dolce`, `arco. f`, `cresc. f`, four more `cresc.`) and three are
music ink. That is the part nobody would have bet on, and it held.

**Surya is silent on 53 of 74 crops that contain readable words.** Finding 5 of
`FINDINGS.md` recorded this failure mode on engraved pages, where it cost one
word and was fixed by upscaling. On a scan it is the dominant term.

Page 22 is the one exception and the one candidate-step failure: 4 candidates
against 7 direction words the layer sees, 0 accepted. Worth opening on its own.

## Surya hallucinated 1307 characters, and the lexicon is what stopped it

On one crop — a `sempre` on page 84, staff 8 — Surya returned:

> SERIALIZATION SERIALIZATION is a strategy to build a serial list of products
> and processes that are not to be consumed on a single basis. […]

repeated to **1307 characters** of fluent invented corporate prose. Surya 2 is a
language model behind an OCR interface (llama.cpp, a 650M GGUF), and on an
ambiguous crop it did what language models do.

`direction_lexicon` refused it, and it cost nothing. But it is worth stating
plainly, because the gate was justified on *arithmetic* — an invented word costs
what a missed one costs — and this is a much stronger reason than the
arithmetic: **the free reader can emit a page of confident nonsense, and the
lexicon is the only thing between that and the exported score.** Anyone tempted
to loosen the gate to buy recall should read this paragraph first.

## Tesseract: refuted as a replacement, shipped as a companion

The obvious lever, and one another session reached independently on a different
Beethoven 5 edition (IMSLP 984073, no text layer): Tesseract reads crops where
Surya is silent. That observation is **correct** — and it is not sufficient,
because reading a crop is not the same as being able to use it.

Both engines on the same 74 crops, the exact images the shipped reader saw:

| rung | crops read | **lexicon-accepted** |
|---|--:|--:|
| Surya (was shipped alone) | 21 | **11** |
| Tesseract, raw | 72 | **5** |
| Tesseract, staff-line fragments stripped | 72 | 11 |
| **Surya OR stripped Tesseract** | 73 | **17** |

**Swapping the rung would have LOST recall**: Tesseract reads three and a half
times as many crops and yields fewer usable words, because its errors are
in-word (`Crese.`, `CTeSC.`, `crese.£`) where Surya's are total. It converts
silence into noise, and the gate then refuses the noise. The measurement that
matters is not "does it read" but "does it read something the lexicon accepts",
and those rank the two engines oppositely.

Much of Tesseract's residue is one character class: `|`, `[`, `]` — staff lines
and barlines crossing the crop and read as punctuation. Stripping those recovers
`'|sempre |'`, `'| cresc.'` and `'[sempre |'` and brings it level with Surya by
a completely different route, which is what makes the union worth more than
either.

### Shipped, and measured on both corpora

`direction_text.default_readers` now asks both, in that order, and each rung
self-disables when its dependency is absent.

| | before (Surya only) | **after (union)** |
|---|--:|--:|
| scan: accepted | 12 | **17** |
| scan: wrong | 0 | **0** |
| scan: recall vs the text layer | 18% | **~37%** |
| engraved benchmark: pooled OMR-NED | 0.1624 | **0.1624** |
| engraved: `wrong direction` | 7 | **7** |

**The engraved benchmark does not move at all**, which is the result that
mattered most for shipping it. Surya already reads every crop there; Tesseract
adds only noise (`'ee ee ee ee ee'`, `'atoll y yt'`, `'(J = 108)'`) and the
lexicon refuses all of it. All fifteen engraved readings are still Surya's, with
zero conflicts. The union buys recall exactly where recall was missing and costs
nothing where it was not.

### What Tesseract actually contributed, which was not what was predicted

The five it supplied were expected to be crops Surya was silent on. Two were.
**Three were repairs** — Surya read the crop and got a letter wrong, the lexicon
refused it, and Tesseract read the same crop correctly:

| page / staff | Surya | Tesseract | kind |
|---|---|---|---|
| 39 / 13 | `''` | `poco rit` | fill |
| 78 / 15 | `''` | `poco` | fill |
| 78 / 12 | `'cresc. )'` | `cresc.` | **repair** |
| 84 / 0 | `'sempire'` | `sempre` | **repair** |
| 84 / 4 | `'senpre'` | `sempre` | **repair** |

So the two rungs are complementary in both directions, not merely
silence-and-fill. That is a better reason for the union than the one it was
adopted on.

### Precision after the union: still nothing invented

All 17 accepted strings are text genuinely present in their own crop, checked by
eye. Sixteen are complete words. The seventeenth, `poco rit` on page 39, is
worth stating exactly because it is easy to mistake for an OCR error: **the crop
itself is clipped** — it contains `poco rita` and no more — so Tesseract read it
faithfully and the truncation is candidate geometry, not reading. It would cost
a few edits against a truth that says `poco ritard.`, against twelve for missing
the mark entirely.

### Cost

**139 ms per crop, 10.3 s for all 74** — Tesseract is a local binary with no
model to load, against Surya's ~9 s per page with a resident server. The union
roughly doubles a page's OCR time and adds no new failure mode: a rung that
throws is caught, recorded in `failed_readers`, and the other one still answers.

One scale note found while timing: handing Surya all 74 crops in a single batch
exceeded its 900 s timeout. The pipeline reads per page (14–26 crops here), so
nothing in the shipped path is near it, but a caller batching a whole score
would be.

## How the numbers were arrived at, including two ways they were wrong first

Recall against a text layer is a floor, not a rate — the layer is another OCR
(it reads `Fag:` for `Fag.` and makes `tJ` out of noteheads), it holds margin
labels and page furniture this reader never looks at, and it counts a word once
per staff so a `sempre` on twelve staves is twelve entries. The 67 is
direction-words whose centre falls inside some staff's own x-range.

Two matching bugs were caught before any of this was believed, both mine:

1. **Matching on x alone.** Reported zero corroboration on page 16 where both
   readers had independently found the same two `cresc.` — a page number four
   staves away shared their column. On a 20-staff page every x is crowded.
   Matching is 2-D now.
2. **Geometric matching without content.** A 3-space window called correct
   readings uncorroborated at 3.8 spaces, because the layer boxes a whole word
   and this reader boxes the letter cluster it kept. The final numbers require
   the letters to agree as well as the position.

Both failures had the same shape as the pooled-versus-per-instance mistake in
`FINDINGS.md` finding 6: a summary statistic that looked authoritative and was
measuring the wrong thing. The crops settled it in both cases.

## What this does not say

- **Nothing here is about a different print.** One edition, five pages, one
  century, one typeface. The engraved benchmark's lesson — that a threshold
  which travels between corpora is rare — applies to this file too.
- **Nothing here measures placement.** A scan has no truth for which beat a mark
  belongs to; every number above is about whether the word is found and read.
- **The 18% is not a bug to be fixed by tuning.** It is one engine being silent
  on legible input. The union above is the measured response; a different vision
  rung (the paid one, already wired for margin labels) is the untested one.
