## Fix 1 — a part's opening clef is not always measure 0's

Ranked item 2. `tools/omr/export.py`.

### The mechanism, which is one step narrower than RESULTS.md states it

RESULTS.md says the export takes each part's clef from measure 0 and therefore
"all fifteen parts export as G2". Read against the file, the MusicXML is
slightly better behaved than that and it does not change the diagnosis: seven
parts open G2 **and then emit a clef change at measure 2** to the clef they
should have opened in (`m1:G2,m2:F4`). The other eight are treble throughout and
correct. LilyPond has no such recovery — `_lily_staff_block` emits one `\clef`
for the whole staff from `staff["clef"]` — so on that exporter all fifteen
Dvořák staves really are treble.

Underneath, `staff["clef"]` is `'treble'` on all fifteen staves, and so is
`measures[0]["clef"]`, while `measures[1..8]["clef"]` carries the right answer
on seven of them. The reason is visible in the detections:

| | measure 0 | measure 1 |
|---|---|---|
| Dvořák staff 3 (Fagotti) | `['structural']` — a brace at conf 0.33 | `['accidental','clef','notehead','rest','structural']` |
| Dvořák staff 1 (Flauto II) | `[]` — nothing at all | `['accidental','clef','rest','structural','time_sig_digit']` |

Every one of the fifteen leading cells holds **no clef detection**: eleven hold
only `structural`, four hold nothing. `transcribe` reads a clef where one is
printed, and system furniture prints none — so measure 0's clef field is the
positional default, and measure 1's is a reading.

### The rule

`export._first_clef_bearing_measure(measures)` returns the index of the first
measure that could have READ a clef or could have USED one:

- it holds a `clef` detection — its clef is a reading, not an inheritance; or
- it holds music (`group_chords_in_measure` is non-empty) — whatever clef was in
  effect there is the clef the exported pitches were resolved under.

Both conditions are load-bearing. Without the first, the rule would overrule a
genuine clef printed at a system head. Without the second it could claim an
opening clef under which notes already written out were not resolved — the
measures it skips emit a whole-measure rest either way, so skipping them cannot
move a pitch. It returns 0 — today's behaviour exactly — when nothing qualifies,
and the whole leading RUN is overridden rather than just measure 0, because
leaving a second furniture cell on the default would emit a clef change back to
it and another one away again.

Applied only where the staff BEGINS a part (`not state`), so a later system's
staff continuing a stitched part is untouched.

### Measured

Export-only, so every row was re-exported from its existing `omr.json` — no
transcription, no CPU window needed.

| row | clefs changed | export |
|---|---|---|
| Dvořák 9 | 7 parts: `m1:G2,m2:F4` → `m1:F4` (×5), `m1:G2,m2:C3` → `m1:C3` (×2) | changed |
| Brahms 1, Beethoven ×2, Mahler 5 | none | **byte-identical** |

Brahms is the control RESULTS.md named — its furniture cell is at the END of the
system, so its measure 0 is genuine and its fourteen clefs including an alto and
a tenor were already right. It stays byte-identical, which is what the rule
firing only on a leading furniture cell should look like.

**Canonical: provably unchanged.** All three engraved works re-export
byte-identically in MusicXML, and `_first_clef_bearing_measure` returns 0 for
every staff of all three — the rule is inert there, not merely neutral. The
LilyPond output is identical with the rule forced off, which is the same thing
said the other way. No canonical run was needed to establish this and none was
spent.

### ⚠️ It costs 0.0032 on the row it fixes, and the reason is item 1

| Dvořák arm | OMR-NED | edits | pred syms | `wrong clef` |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| A baseline | 0.5873 | 955 | 834 | 8 |
| B **fix 1 only** | **0.5905** | 956 | 827 | 12 |
| C fix 4 only (measure 0 dropped) | 0.5665 | 895 | 788 | 4 |
| D fix 1 + fix 4 | 0.5665 | 895 | 788 | 4 |

Arms C and D were previewed by deleting measure 0 from the JSON before export
(`scratchpad`, not committed) — the real fix 4 is measured below.

Two things to read off it. **`wrong clef` gets worse while the clefs get
right**: the spurious measure is still there, so musicdiff aligns our measure 2
against truth's measure 1, and moving the clef declaration back into measure 1
moves it into the bar that alignment discards. The clef is now correct in a bar
the metric does not look at. **And D equals C exactly** — with the furniture cell
gone, `_first_clef_bearing_measure` returns 0 on every Dvořák staff and fix 1 is
a no-op on this corpus.

So fix 1 is **dominated by fix 4 on the only row that can test it**, and this is
recorded rather than hidden. It is kept for three reasons that the pooled figure
cannot express:

1. the exported file is notationally correct — seven parts stop claiming a
   treble clef the page does not print, which is what a human opening the
   MusicXML sees, and the same trade CLAUDE.md records for chord order
   (`+2 edits, 46 notes out of the wrong class`);
2. **LilyPond is not covered by fix 4's benefit at all in the general case** and
   has no clef-change to recover with — there, a leading furniture cell is the
   difference between `\clef bass` and `\clef treble` for the whole staff;
3. it is the standing answer for any leading-furniture cell that fix 4's
   deliberately conservative content test declines to remove.

The 7 fewer predicted symbols with 1 more edit is also, for the record, the
symmetry trap RESULTS.md documents in the resolution arm — the denominator moved
further than the numerator.

Tests: `tools/omr/tests/test_export.py::TestFirstClefBearingMeasure` (8) and
`::TestOpeningClefInExport` (3), including the two cases the rule must NOT fire
on — a leading bar holding music, and a tacet leading bar holding its whole rest.

## Fix 2 — the margin crop still clips, on four publishers out of five

Ranked item 5. `tools/omr/staff_labels_vision.py`.

### First, a correction to the work order

RESULTS.md attributes `ken in C u. G`, `larinetten in B` and `competen in C` to
the Dvořák Simrock row. They are **Brahms**, Breitkopf *Sämtliche Werke*.
Dvořák's row had **zero** unresolved labels. The three rows that carried
rejected labels were Brahms (6), Beethoven 984073 (2) and Mahler (3).

### Why the 2026-08-31 fix did not travel

It was measured on **one publisher**. `crops-w14/20/26` are twelve systems of
Beethoven 5 and Beethoven 6, and the conclusion "20 is the smaller change that
gets the whole benefit" is true of that corpus and of nothing else.

`probe_margin_reach.py` (new, in this directory) measures the *printing* rather
than the reader: for each staff it walks left from the crop's own `x_ref` and
reports the margin ink runs in staff spacings — the unit `MARGIN_SPACINGS` is
written in, and the only one that transfers between a 2897-px scan and a
5409-px one. Over the five scan pages, 74 staves:

| edition | furthest label | staves the 20-spacing crop cuts |
|---|--:|--:|
| Dvořák, Simrock 1894 | 20.3 | 3 of 15 |
| Beethoven 5, IMSLP 575951 | 22.5 | 2 of 12 |
| Beethoven 5, IMSLP 984073 | 22.9 | 2 of 12 |
| Brahms 1, Breitkopf | 24.9 | 6 of 14 |
| Mahler 5, Peters | 26.4 | 11 of 19 |

**Twenty cuts a label on all five, including the edition the fix was made on.**
The 14 → 20 move fixed the failures its corpus *reported* without checking
whether ink still crossed the boundary — and on Beethoven 984073 it still did,
on exactly the two staves whose labels this benchmark then found rejected.

The correspondence is one-to-one, which is what makes this a diagnosis rather
than a plausible story:

| printed | reach | arrived as |
|---|--:|---|
| Pauken in C u.G (Brahms staff 8) | 24.4 | `ken in C u. G` |
| 2 Klarinetten in B (staff 2) | 24.9 | `larinetten in B` |
| 2 Trompeten in C (staff 7) | 24.6 | `competen in C` |
| Kontrafagott (staff 4) | 22.2 | `contrafagott` |
| Clarinetti in B. (Beethoven staff 2) | 22.3 | `Carinetti in B.` |
| Timpani in C.G. (Beethoven staff 6) | 22.9 | `Compani in C.G.` |

`Carinetti` looked like an internal OCR slip and is not one — staff 2 is cut at
20 and the `Cl` ligature is what the crop lands on.

### The rule: 20 → 30, placed in a measured empty band

The probe searches **45** spacings out and finds nothing at all beyond 26.4 on
any of the 74 staves. So there is an 18-spacing gap with the entire corpus on
one side, and 30 sits in it — the same shape of decision as
`STEM_MAX_HEIGHT_LINES`, not a bump to make one case pass.

Widening costs no resolution: `build_margin_crop` downscales on
`max(width, height)` and a system's crop is far taller than wide, so the scale
factor does not move.

### Measured

**Validation corpus (`benchmarks/omr-margin-labels-2026-08`), 12 systems, 107
staves.** New `crops-w30` + `results-surya-w30.json`, generated with the same
`--limit 12 --pages 10,40,59` as the existing widths:

| reader | agree | disagree | recovered | missed | both silent | acc |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| w20 | 32 | 3 | 16 | 1 | 55 | 91% |
| w26 | 32 | 3 | 16 | 1 | 55 | 91% |
| **w30** | **32** | **3** | **16** | **1** | **55** | **91%** |

Not merely equal in tally — **Surya returns character-identical labels on all
twelve crops at 20, 26 and 30**. This change is free on the corpus that set the
old constant.

⚠️ **The committed w14/w20/w26 table in SURYA_BAKEOFF_2026-08-31.md no longer
reproduces.** Re-scoring the same committed results files today gives w14 =
33/2 and w20 = 32/3 where the doc records w14 = 32/3 and w20 = 33/2 — the
ordering that justified 14 → 20 has *reversed*. `score_readers.py` re-resolves
truth through `instruments.lookup` at scoring time by design, and the lexicon
changed on 2026-08-31 (`Tr. Alt.`); the one staff that moved is on
`beethoven-symphony-5_p59_s0` and the w14/w20 disagreements there are a
row-assignment shift, not an OCR difference. At n=35 with one crop able to move
the tally by 1, **this harness cannot separate 14 from 20 from 30 on accuracy**
— which is why the case for 30 rests on the printing measurement, and why the
harness is used here only to show the change costs nothing.

**Scan rows — labels, which is what moved:**

| row | labelled staves | unresolved labels | from score order |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dvořák 9 | 12 → **15 of 15** | 0 → 0 | 3 → 0 |
| Brahms 1 | 8 → **12 of 14** | 6 → **2** | 6 → 2 |
| Beethoven 984073 | 10 → **11 of 12** | 2 → **0** | 2 → 1 |
| Mahler 5 | 15 → **16** | 3 → **1** | 4 → 3 |
| **total** | **45 → 54** | **11 → 3** | 15 → 6 |

The three that remain are not clipping: two are Surya rendering stacked

trumpets in two keys) and one is `Contrafagott`, now read perfectly and absent

work, recorded here and not chased.

`instruments_from_score_order` falling 15 → 6 is the same fact from the other
side: fewer staves need the positional fallback because more were read outright.

**Scan rows — OMR-NED, which did not move at all.** Same tree, w20 arm against
w30 arm, both run tonight:

| row | w20 | w30 |
|---|--:|--:|
| Beethoven 984073 | 0.7119 / 1305 | 0.7119 / 1305 |
| Dvořák 9 | 0.5907 / 957 | 0.5907 / 957 |
| Brahms 1 | 0.9352 / 3692 | 0.9352 / 3692 |
| Mahler 5 | 0.8412 / 1684 | 0.8412 / 1684 |

**Identical to the edit on every row**, while nine more staves learned what
instrument they are. The Mahler arms are the sharpest case: they read different
labels (`surya 15 + tesseract 4` against `surya 17`), applied a different number
of clefs (4 against 5), produced different MusicXML — and scored the same to the
edit. Part naming does not reach OMR-NED, which CLAUDE.md already records; what
this adds is that the clef corrections riding on the labels did not either, here.

So this fix is justified by the label yield and by nothing else, and that is
stated rather than dressed up. Its value is downstream — the part-staff join,
the dossier pin, and the instrument-driven clef correction all run on labels.

**Canonical: not run.** The change cannot reach it. `orchestral_eval` renders
engraved fixtures that carry no margin labels at all, and the label tier is
`text_layer: 0, surya: 0` on all three; a wider margin crop of a page with
nothing in its margin returns the same nothing.
